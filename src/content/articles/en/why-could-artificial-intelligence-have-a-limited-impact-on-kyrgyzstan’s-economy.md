Why Could Artificial Intelligence Have a Limited Impact on Kyrgyzstan’s Economy?

Artificial intelligence is increasingly becoming part of the global economy, but its economic benefits may vary significantly from one country to another. According to the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development’s (EBRD) Transition Report 2026–2027, Kyrgyzstan is among the five economies expected to gain the least from AI adoption in terms of labour productivity growth.

The report estimates that moderate adoption of AI technologies could contribute an additional 0.1 percentage points to Kyrgyzstan’s annual labour productivity growth over the next 10 years. This is the fifth-lowest result among the economies covered by the study. Only Benin, Côte d’Ivoire, Nigeria and Senegal recorded lower projected gains.

Three AI adoption scenarios

The EBRD assessed the potential impact of AI on the economy under three different adoption scenarios:

- Slow adoption: an additional 0.03 percentage points in annual productivity growth.
- Moderate adoption: an additional 0.1 percentage points.
- Fast adoption: an additional 0.2 percentage points.

These figures represent the potential additional contribution of AI to labour productivity growth in Kyrgyzstan.

Why could the economic benefits be limited?

One of the main reasons is the structure of the country’s economy and employment market.

AI has significant potential to improve productivity in activities such as information processing, programming, document preparation and data analysis. Economies with a larger share of these activities may be better positioned to benefit from AI technologies.

However, access to technology alone is not enough. Digital infrastructure, computing capacity, skilled professionals and businesses’ ability to integrate new technologies into their operations are also essential factors.

Where does Uzbekistan stand?

Uzbekistan was not separately listed in the EBRD’s specific labour productivity projections. Therefore, its exact position in this comparison cannot be determined.

However, the country has recorded progress in another international assessment. According to information published by the Press Service of the President of Uzbekistan in August 2026, Uzbekistan climbed 25 positions in the Global AI Readiness Index to rank 62nd.

It is important to distinguish between the two assessments. The Global AI Readiness Index measures a country’s preparedness for artificial intelligence, while the EBRD report estimates AI’s potential contribution to labour productivity growth.

The EBRD’s findings highlight that economic benefits from artificial intelligence depend on more than simply having access to technology. A country’s ability to integrate AI into production, services and business operations, alongside investments in infrastructure and human capital, will play a major role in determining its future economic growth.
